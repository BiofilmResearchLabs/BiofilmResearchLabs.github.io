Biofilm Research Laboratories · Penn Dental Medicine

Koo Research Group

We study how harmful biofilms form and cause disease, then use what we learn to design better ways to detect, reach, and treat them. The group combines microbiology with imaging, materials, nanotechnology, and robotics, with a strong focus on problems that are difficult to solve with conventional treatment.

Research

01

Pathogenic Biofilm Biology & Spatial Ecology

How do microbial interactions and spatial organization enable biofilms to cause disease?

Biofilms are structured communities, not just layers of microbes. We study how cells, extracellular matrix, and the surrounding environment are organized in space, and how that organization helps biofilms persist and cause disease.

02

Nanozymes & Catalytic Therapeutics

Can engineered catalytic materials selectively disrupt pathogenic biofilms?

We design catalytic nanomaterials that can act directly inside biofilms. A major focus is nanozymes — materials with enzyme-like activity that can generate antimicrobial effects where they are needed.

03

Microrobotics & Adaptive Biofilm Intervention

How can robotic systems reach and treat biofilms in places conventional approaches cannot?

Some biofilms sit in irregular or confined spaces that are difficult to reach. We build small-scale robotic systems that can move through these environments, deliver treatment, disrupt biofilms, and help remove them.

04

Precision Diagnostics & Translation

How can we turn biofilm research into better tools for diagnosis and treatment?

We work to turn biofilm biology and engineering into practical tools for sampling, detection, and treatment, and test them in models that better reflect real oral disease.

How We Work

01

Understanding the Biofilm

Microbiology · imaging · quantitative analysis

We use microbiology and imaging to learn how pathogenic biofilms are built and how they behave.

02

Engineering the Intervention

Nanotechnology · materials science · catalysis · robotics

We use catalytic materials, nanotechnology, and robotics to create systems that can reach and act on biofilms with greater precision.

03

Moving Toward Translation

Disease models · interdisciplinary collaboration

We test promising approaches in increasingly realistic disease models, with the goal of moving useful technologies closer to oral healthcare.

Selected Discoveries

Microrobots can work across the mouth’s difficult spaces

Magnetic microrobots can move across different oral surfaces and through confined spaces, including areas that are difficult to reach with conventional tools. The work points toward more targeted ways to deliver treatment, collect samples, and remove biofilm.

Read the discovery ↗

Caries is shaped by where microbes live

Three-dimensional mapping of dental plaque revealed that cavity-associated microbes occupy distinct spatial neighborhoods. Their location helps create acidic microenvironments linked to enamel damage.

Read the discovery ↗

A robot that can kill, break down, and remove biofilm

Catalytic antimicrobial robots combine chemical activity with magnetic control to kill bacteria, break apart the protective biofilm matrix, and physically remove the remaining material.
