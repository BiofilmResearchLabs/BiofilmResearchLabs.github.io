Biofilm Research Laboratories · Penn Dental Medicine

Ren Research Group

We study how microbes move through their surroundings, where they settle, how they build communities, and what those communities go on to do. By combining microbiology with imaging, biophysics, computation, and disease models, we connect events that begin with individual cells to the organization and behavior of whole microbial communities.

Research

01

Structure, Propulsion & Movement

How do microbial shape and propulsion determine the ways cells move?

Microbes come in many shapes and use different machinery to move. We ask how those physical features produce different ways of swimming, reversing, turning, and exploring their surroundings.

02

Navigation, Colonization & Persistence

How do microbes find, reach, and remain in the places where communities form?

Moving is only useful if a microbe can reach and remain in the right place. We study how surfaces, confinement, flow, neighboring cells, and local conditions affect navigation, trapping, escape, and colonization.

03

Spatial Organization & Community Assembly

How do individual microbial behaviors scale into organized communities?

Microbial communities emerge from many local interactions. We use high-throughput 3D imaging and quantitative analysis to see how individual cells and naturally occurring aggregates come together to build organized biofilms.

04

Community Function & Disease

How does spatial organization change what microbial communities do?

The same microbes can behave differently depending on where they are and who surrounds them. We study how spatial arrangement, microbial interactions, and local conditions change community activity, virulence, and disease.

How We Work

01

Seeing Microbes as They Really Are

Live · native-state · super-resolution · 3D imaging

We use advanced microscopy and high-throughput 3D imaging to see cells and communities in their native spatial setting rather than relying on a few selected snapshots.

02

Testing the Physical Rules

Experiments, quantitative analysis, and simulations help us test how cell shape, propulsion, surfaces, and confinement produce the behaviors we observe.

03

Connecting Behavior to Community Function

We combine imaging with computation, molecular measurements, and disease models to ask how cell-level behavior changes the organization and activity of whole communities.
