BIOFILM RESEARCH LABORATORIES · UNIVERSITY OF PENNSYLVANIA

How microbes move, organize, and drive disease.

We study how microbes find a place to settle, build organized communities, and become harmful — and use what we learn to develop better ways to study and treat biofilms.

A scientific premise

From single cells
to microbial communities.

A biofilm starts with individual cells, but much of its biology emerges after they come together. We follow that process from movement and colonization to community structure, microbial interactions, and disease.

Caries is shaped by where microbes live

Three-dimensional mapping showed that microbes in dental plaque occupy distinct spatial neighborhoods linked to local acidity and tooth decay.

Read the story ↗

Bacterial-fungal communities move as collectives

Microbes from human saliva can form mixed bacterial–fungal groups that move across surfaces as a unit, giving the community behaviors individual cells do not have on their own.

Read the story ↗

A hidden partner can intensify childhood caries

Selenomonas sputigena can work with Streptococcus mutans to reorganize dental plaque and increase its disease-causing activity.

Read the story ↗

Microrobots can work across the mouth’s difficult spaces

Adaptive microrobots can move across different oral surfaces and through confined spaces, creating new possibilities for targeted treatment, delivery, and removal.
