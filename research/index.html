01Navigation

Microbial Navigation & Colonization

Microbes have to get to the right place before they can settle and build a community.

Scientific visual forthcoming

2022·Navigation

Bacterial-fungal communities move as collectives

Bacteria attached to fungi in human saliva can form mobile groups that move across tooth-like surfaces together. The assembled community gains behaviors that its individual members do not show on their own.

02Assembly

Biofilm Assembly & Spatial Organization

Biofilms do not start as random piles of cells.

Scientific visual forthcoming

2022·Assembly

Some biofilms begin as communities, not single cells

Microbes in human saliva often arrive at a surface already assembled into mixed-species groups. These natural aggregates can become starting points for the growth of organized biofilms.

Explore the story ↗Related publication ↗

2020·Assembly

Caries is shaped by where microbes live

Three-dimensional mapping of dental plaque revealed that cavity-associated microbes occupy distinct spatial neighborhoods. Their location helps create acidic microenvironments linked to enamel damage.

Microbial Interactions, Function & Disease

A hidden partner can intensify childhood caries

Selenomonas sputigena can work with Streptococcus mutans to reorganize dental plaque, creating structures that concentrate acid production and make tooth decay more severe.

04Intervention

Precision Biofilm Intervention & Diagnostics

Biofilms can be difficult to reach and hard to remove.

Scientific visual forthcoming

2025·Intervention

Microrobots can work across the mouth’s difficult spaces

Magnetic microrobots can move across different oral surfaces and through confined spaces, including areas that are difficult to reach with conventional tools. The work points toward more targeted ways to deliver treatment, collect samples, and remove biofilm.

Explore the story ↗Related publication ↗

2025·Intervention

Nanozyme microcapsules reach confined infection sites

Magnetic microcapsules combine controlled movement with nanozyme activity, allowing them to travel through narrow, branched spaces and deliver antimicrobial action directly to biofilms.

Explore the story ↗Related publication ↗

2019·Intervention

A robot that can kill, break down, and remove biofilm

Catalytic antimicrobial robots combine chemical activity with magnetic control to kill bacteria, break apart the protective biofilm matrix, and physically remove the remaining material.

Explore the story ↗Related publication ↗
